What Is Business Process Automation?

What is business process automation, in plain terms? It is the use of software to run the repeatable steps of a business process without someone doing them by hand: a supplier invoice is read, checked and filed, a new lead lands in the CRM and gets a first reply, a weekly report builds itself. The process stays yours. The copying, checking and chasing go to the machine.

Four terms get mixed up in vendor pitches:

  • Business process automation (BPA): the whole process, end to end, across every tool it touches.
  • Robotic process automation (RPA): software robots that click through a screen the way a person would. Handy for old software with no API, fragile as soon as the screen changes.
  • Workflow automation: apps connected so that one event triggers the next step, with tools like Zapier, Make or n8n.
  • AI automation: a model added where fixed rules fall short, to read an email, sort a document or rank a signal.

A good provider mixes them per process, not per trend. And the hard part is rarely the tool. It is integration:

  • software with no API, which pushes you toward RPA and its fragility;
  • messy or duplicated data, which an automation copies faster, errors included;
  • access rights and personal data under GDPR, to settle before anything runs;
  • silent failures, the run that reports "success" while writing nothing.

A diagnostic exists to find these before you pay for a build.
