Getting visible in ChatGPT, Perplexity and AI answers: the GEO guide

In short

A growing share of your customers no longer type "plumber Lyon" into Google: they ask ChatGPT or Perplexity to recommend someone. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) means making sure the answer mentions you. Three projects, in order: let AI crawlers in, make your website readable and citable for them, then build the reputation that earns their trust. This guide connects our four detailed articles on the subject.

Your customers changed doors

For twenty years the question was "am I ranking well on Google?". It still stands, but a second one has settled in next to it: "when someone asks an AI to recommend a provider like me, do I exist in the answer?". ChatGPT counts hundreds of millions of weekly users, Perplexity is establishing itself as an answer engine, and Google itself now answers above its own results with AI Overviews.

The fundamental difference: a Google results page shows ten links, an AI answer cites two or three names. There is no page 2. Either you are in the answer, or you do not exist.

How AIs pick who to cite

The models draw on two sources: what they learned during training (masses of public text where your brand appears, or does not), and what they fetch live from the web at question time. On the first source you act slowly, through reputation. On the second you act fast: your website just has to be accessible, readable and trustworthy for their crawlers.

Why your brand is unknown to the models today, and where they get what they know: our article on why ChatGPT does not know your brand lays out the full diagnosis.

Project 1: let the AI crawlers in

First thing to check: do your robots.txt and your firewall let GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and Google-Extended through? Many hosts and security plugins block them by default, with an understandable protective logic but a direct commercial effect: an AI that cannot read your website will never cite you on fresh questions.

The list of crawlers, what each one feeds and the exact lines to add: blocking or welcoming AI crawlers.

Project 2: be readable and citable

An AI crawler that enters your website needs to understand quickly: who you are, what you do, for whom, where. Two tools work for you:

  • The llms.txt file, a plain-text summary at the root of your website that tells AIs what to read and what to remember. It is the little brother of robots.txt, aimed at comprehension rather than permission. The format, examples and pitfalls: the complete llms.txt guide. You can also generate yours in two minutes with our free generator.
  • Content that answers precise questions, with dated, quantified, attributed statements. AIs cite what they can extract cleanly: a page structured around sharp questions beats a vague presentation text.

The four conditions to meet, in order, to go from "readable" to "cited": GEO, how to get cited by ChatGPT and Perplexity.

Project 3: the reputation that builds trust

AIs cross-check. A business only cited by itself weighs less than one mentioned by a professional directory, the local press, customer reviews and partners. This groundwork is the same as classic SEO authority building. If that project is your priority, our guide on building recognition and earning Google's trust covers it in full; everything in it also serves your AI visibility.

What about Google in all this?

Google is no bystander: its AI Overviews answer directly at the top of the results, citing a handful of sources. The pages that earn their spot look a lot like the ones ChatGPT cites: a direct answer to the question, clear structure, identifiable source. Who loses traffic, who gains, and how to become the cited source rather than the casualty: AI Overviews in France, what changes for your visibility.

Measure yourself honestly

Three checks to run this month, in this order:

  1. Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity to recommend a provider in your trade and your city. Note who comes up, and whether you are in.
  2. Check access: robots.txt, firewall, llms.txt present and up to date.
  3. Repeat the test in three months, same questions, and compare.

Frequent questions

What is GEO exactly?

Generative Engine Optimization: making your business visible in the answers that AI engines write, the way SEO makes it visible in Google results. The raw material is the same public web, but the levers differ: AI crawler access, a plainly stated identity, structured data, an llms.txt file, citable passages and consistent external mentions.

Does GEO replace SEO?

No, it sits on top of it. AI engines read the same pages Google crawls, so a website with broken foundations is invisible to both. Fix the SEO basics first, then add the GEO layer: it is additional work, not replacement work.

How do I know if AI engines already cite my business?

Ask them the question your prospects ask: recommend a provider of your trade in your city, in ChatGPT and Perplexity. If you are absent, run the free diagnostic: it measures your AI crawler access, llms.txt, structured data and citable passages in 30 seconds.
