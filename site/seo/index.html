SEO

Get found on Google

Most small business websites are not invisible because of their competitors. They are invisible because of measurable, fixable problems: pages Google cannot read, titles that say nothing, content that answers no real question. This is where we collect what we know about fixing that.

What actually moves the needle

SEO advice tends to drown small business owners in tactics. In practice, four families of work carry nearly all of the result, and they come in order:

  • Technical health. Google must be able to find, read and trust your pages: indexing, speed, one clear title and description per page, structured data, a valid sitemap. These fixes cost days, not months, and unblock everything else.
  • Content that answers real questions. One page per question your customers actually ask, written to be the most useful answer available. Ten solid pages beat sixty thin ones, and thin pages now actively hurt the whole domain.
  • Local visibility. For a business that serves an area: a consistent name, address and phone everywhere, a maintained Google listing, and reviews that keep coming.
  • Trust signals. A valid certificate, security headers, authentic email configuration, identifiable authors. Google reads these as marks of a serious operation, and so do the AI engines.

Everything we publish here follows one rule: every claim maps to something you can measure on your own website. Our audit engine runs 161 checks across these exact families, so each guide tells you what the check looks at, what a good value is, and how to fix a bad one.

Frequent questions

Where should a small business start with SEO?

With a measurement, not with an opinion. Before writing a single line of content, know whether Google can read your website: indexing, one clear title per page, loading speed, working sitemap. These are the fixes that cost days and unblock everything else. Content and authority come after, and take months.

Does SEO still matter now that people ask AI engines?

More than ever, because AI engines read the same web Google does. A website that is well structured, fast and clearly written feeds both Google rankings and AI answers. The AI side has its own additional levers, which we cover in our GEO guides.

How do you know if an SEO provider is serious?

Ask for measured facts and verify three of them yourself. A serious audit names the file, the tag and the target value for every finding, and survives spot checks. A grade with no breakdown measures nothing. Our guide on what a useful audit looks like shows exactly how to check.
